We have received the following from Giancarlo Carreño Arce (Universidad Nacional de Música, Lima, Peru).

November 9, 2026 marks the 168th birthday of José María Valle-Riestra, who is considered one of the most important composers in Peruvian history. He was among the first to incorporate Pre-Hispanic and Andean musical elements into a large-scale project such as an opera. His contribution to music education in Peru is also very significant. His theoretical writings helped to modernize musical training in the country.

José María Valle-Riestra was born in Lima, Peru, in 1858. He began his music training at the age of nine, when his parents took him to London. However, the most significant period in his education was between 1895 and 1897, when he had the opportunity to study counterpoint, fugue, and orchestration at the Paris Conservatoire with André Gédalge, the influential music teacher who was also known for his role in the musical training of composers such as Florent Schmitt and Maurice Ravel.
 
Upon his return to Peru in late 1897 and inspired by the nationalist trend that emerged in the aftermath of the war with Chile, he began to work alongside librettist Federico Blume on the opera _Ollanta_ (RISM ID no. 1001379753; [RISM Catalog](https://opac.rism.info/id/rismid/rism1001379753?sid=100988591){:blank} \| [RISM Online](https://rism.online/sources/1001379753){:blank. This dramatic work tells a tragic love story set in the ancient Inca Empire, and its music features traditional Peruvian genres – such as the yaraví and the cashua – blended with classical European musical forms. The opera premiered in 1900 with limited success; it was revived in 1920 with new musical arrangements and a revised libretto (RISM ID no. 1001382242; [RISM Online](https://rism.online/sources/1001382242){:blank}) and was met with critical and popular acclaim.

<small>Manuscript of Ollanta, held at the Universidad Nacional de Música (UNM), Lima, Peru. Courtesy of @ Universidad Nacional de Música (UNM) \| Giancarlo Carreño (photograph).</small>

Valle-Riestra’s catalog includes his unfinished opera _Atahualpa_ (RISM ID no. 1001381015; [RISM Catalog](https://opac.rism.info/id/rismid/rism1001381015?sid=100988591){:blank}\ | [RISM Online](https://rism.online/sources/1001381015){:blank}), several orchestral pieces, a few Lieder, the motet _O salutaris_ (RISM ID no. 1001367006; [RISM Catalog](https://opac.rism.info/id/rismid/rism1001367006?sid=100988591){:blank}\ | [RISM Online](https://rism.online/sources/1001367006){:blank}), and a highly acclaimed Requiem Mass (RISM ID no. 1001389868; [RISM Online](https://rism.online/sources/1001389868){:blank}). Many of his manuscripts are held by the Biblioteca Nacional del Peru (Library siglum: PE-Lbnp; [RISM Catalog](https://opac.rism.info/id/rismauthorities/rismks30080991){:blank}\ | [RISM Online](https://rism.online/institutions/30080991){:blank} and the library of the Universidad Nacional de Música (Library siglum: PE-Lcnm; [RISM Catalog](https://opac.rism.info/id/rismauthorities/rismks30080992){:blank}\ | [RISM Online](https://rism.online/institutions/30080992){:blank}, originally known as the Academia Nacional de Música, where Valle-Riestra served as deputy director. These sources are currently being incorporated into RISM, and digital copies of many of his works are available through the RISM records ([RISM Online](https://rism.online/search?q=valle-riestra&nc=PE&mode=sources&page=1&rows=40){:blank}\ | [RISM Catalog](https://opac.rism.info/rism/Search/Results?lookfor=Valle-Riestra){:blank}).
 
José María Valle-Riestra sought to preserve elements of Peruvian culture in his music. His musical legacy continues to be rediscovered by researchers and contemporary musicians who acknowledge his contribution to the national heritage and his influence on the development of academic music in Peru.
